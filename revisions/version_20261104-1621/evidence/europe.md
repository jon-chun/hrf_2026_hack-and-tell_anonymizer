# European media, society and residency evidence

Accessed 4 November 2026. The structured inventory contains 20 Creative Europe awards, 20 OSF-network grants/fellowship projects, and 20 publicly disclosed ECPMF residency-support cases. These instrument types are intentionally distinct; residency alumni are not software-development grantees.

## Creative Europe — Journalism Partnerships

The closest sampled project is NEXT-IJ: journalism tools and training delivered through a transnational partnership. CJEGC is another data/collaboration comparator; ENACT supplies an accessibility/plain-language comparator. The broad pattern is a shared sector intervention with news organizations, capacity-building and reuse across borders, rather than an isolated developer's product. This is an inference from the portfolio, not a finding that these features caused selection. [2024-call results](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/winners-fourth-call-proposals-journalism-partnerships-announced), [2022-call results](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/second-set-eu-supported-journalism-partnerships-help-wider-news-sector).

The historical 2025 Collaborations call required three partners in three participating countries, allowed up to 80% co-financing, and excluded most natural-person applications. These are historical conditions; do not replace them with the 90% associated with a different topic. No claim is made that a current call is open. [Official historical call](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/funding/call-proposals-journalism-partnerships-collaborations-2025).

For the anonymizer concept, develop a genuine consortium workstream: participating newsroom workflow, language coverage, verification of editorial/source protection, integration and training. A software feature list needs an explicit route to better or more sustainable reporting. The public award sources name consortium organizations, not the individual grant managers. The 2022-call account identifies independent expert review, without naming evaluators. An organization's first position in the partner list is not evidence that its CEO led the award.

IJ4EU and Journalismfund Europe appear as EC consortium beneficiaries while also making downstream awards in this dataset. Treat these as a funding-chain relationship rather than independent votes for the same concept. Pluralism/regranting awards are not equivalent to a newsroom software grant. [2023-call results](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/third-generation-eu-supported-journalism-partnerships-ready-help-news-sector).

## Open Society Foundations

OSF's organizational approach is relevant to a civic-use implementation partner: most grants result from direct approaches by program staff, and support may be organizational or project-specific. An open public award history is not a universal open application route. Confirm the actual program and invitation process before adapting a proposal. [How OSF funds](https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/how-we-work/how-we-fund), [application FAQ](https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/grants/faq?mobileformat=false&muraadminpreview=).

The sample deliberately spans programs rather than claiming a single stable OSF preference. Eight historical Hungarian awards show support for media, documentation, civic institutions and rights; the CEU Aaron Swartz fellowship is a digital-evidence adjacency, not anonymization precedent. Eight Justice fellow projects are specifically criminal-legal reform initiatives. Three Access Now grants and a Factchequeado award supply organizational and journalism comparators. The latter supports Spanish-language collaboration and training; that is a stronger multilingual adoption lesson than a claim that any particular NLP method is preferred. [Hungarian awards](https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/uploads/f6701cde-300a-4d8a-adf3-e6ee2e47266a/open-society-hungarian-grantees-en-20170303.pdf), [2024 Justice cohort](https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/newsroom/open-society-foundations-announce-2024-soros-justice-fellows), [Access Now disclosures](https://www.accessnow.org/financials/), [Factchequeado disclosure](https://factchequeado.com/institucional/20240823/open-society-foundation-grant/).

Mary Fitzgerald's 2021 appointment and 2023 Expression role document an institutional intersection of journalism, technology and rights. Christina Voight and Adam Culbreath are named in the 2024 Justice announcement. None is established as the selector for a specific award. The 2024 presidency transition provides historical governance context, not a personal persuasion target. Avoid inferring private motivations, personal political preferences or current contact responsibility. [Fitzgerald appointment](https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/newsroom/open-society-names-mary-fitzgerald-to-lead-information-democracy-work), [Expression statement](https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/voices/how-expression-inspires-change), [presidency transition](https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/newsroom/binaifer-nowrojee-appointed-new-president-of-open-society-foundations-mark-malloch-brown-to-step-down).

Recommended communication: start with which civic actors can safely do what after the project, and the partner organization's ability to sustain it. Offer a concrete mechanism, access path and accountable outcomes; do not lead with an unspecific claim that “AI anonymization transforms democracy.” Justice fellowships should not be presented as a generic software fund. This advice is analyst inference.

## ECPMF / MFRR

JiR is an assistance instrument for threatened media professionals. Its central outputs are safety, recovery and ability to continue journalism. The gallery establishes that the named individuals participated, but does not provide most exact dates, amounts or private project proposals. The inventory preserves those unknowns. The published residency benefits are program-wide, not attributed individual grant amounts. [JiR program](https://www.ecpmf.eu/support/journalists-in-residence/), [public alumni](https://www.ecpmf.eu/support/journalists-in-residence/jir-alumni/).

Sami Menteş's own profile dates his residency to 2023/24; Michelle Demishevich's archived profile dates hers to 2017/18. These are unusually specific public case details. Alina Toropova is documented as program manager in a 2025 partner interview; this does not establish her role in every past selection. [Menteş](https://www.ecpmf.eu/support/journalists-in-residence/jir-alumni/sami-mentes/), [Demishevich](https://archive.ecpmf.eu/help-for-journalists/journalists-in-residence/alumni/michelle-demishevich.html), [manager interview](https://sheltercity.org/shelter-city-leipzig-is-opening-its-doors-to-journalists-at-risk/).

For a software developer, JiR is not an appropriate request merely because the tool protects journalists. A potential route is a separate implementation/training partnership or eligible media-capacity program, subject to its exact call rules. If an actual applicant needs relocation, describe verified journalistic work and the associated threat rather than reframing a software roadmap as emergency need. This is a program-fit judgment, not a prediction about individual awards.
