# Journalism-funder evidence dossier

Researched 2026-11-04 from first-party funder pages. This is a bounded evidence sample, not a claim that the listed grants are every grant these organizations have ever made. The published award pages routinely withhold project-level details to protect investigations; absence of a feature in a listing is **not** evidence that it was absent from the application or work.

The comparison concept is provisional: a locally run tool for consistent, reversible pseudonymization of sensitive data in long-form text. No applicant, country, budget, delivery plan, or actual proposal was supplied.

## Fund for Investigative Journalism (FIJ)

**Documented program/selection evidence.** FIJ funds investigations across print, online, broadcast, books, documentaries and podcasts. Regular grants reach $10,000; seed grants are $1,000–$2,500. Full proposals need a U.S. outlet commitment; foreign reporting needs a strong U.S. angle and U.S. English-language publication. The board seeks new reporting that exposes wrongdoing or misuse of power. Its FAQ asks for the central question, novelty, reporting roadmap, source access, preliminary findings, and why the applicant is suited to deliver. A detailed, justified reporting-expense budget is required. Staff screen and the board votes on eligible proposals. [Application page](https://fij.org/apply-for-a-grant/) [FAQ](https://fij.org/grants-faq/)

**Publicly named selection people.** The 2025 public announcement names Executive Director Eric Ferrero; it says the Board reviews/votes and names Bill Marimow, Ellen Weiss, Alan Berlow, Anu Narayanswamy, and Diana Jean Schemo as the seed-review group. It does not identify which person chose an individual award. [Announcement](https://fij.org/fund-awards-record-49-new-grants-to-journalists-across-the-u-s/)

**Narrative implication.** A software proposal is a poor direct fit unless framed as reporting infrastructure attached to one concrete, publication-committed investigation that uncovers a defined abuse of power. The proposal should show preliminary reporting, editorial demand, a named US publication path, and the direct reporting cost/risk that privacy-preserving handling solves. This is an inference from FIJ’s stated criteria, not a causal result from its winners.

## Journalismfund Europe (JFE)

**Documented program/selection evidence.** The European Cross-border Grants programme is for teams of at least two professional journalists/news outlets from two European countries; at least 80% of the requested budget must go to EU-country journalists/media. The jury evaluates expert-led review, added value to mainstream coverage, social relevance, newsworthiness, research/storytelling originality, feasibility, team capability, work effort, cross-border structure and EU watchdog value, then considers portfolio diversity. It uses rotating independent juries whose members remain anonymous during their mandate. [Programme page](https://www.journalismfund.eu/european-cross-border-grants) [criteria and process](https://grants.journalismfund.eu/en/european-cross-border-grants-programme-grant-details)

The small-newsroom 2025 round explicitly selected projects for media development/innovation, organizational strategy responding to audience needs, and ability to produce robust journalism. Awards were up to €5,000 with a three-month completion period. The public page calls its four-person jury anonymous; no individual selection staff are named. [Award announcement](https://www.journalismfund.eu/news/media-innovation-boosted-across-europe-26-projects)

**Narrative implication.** The provisional tool fits this funder only if an eligible cross-border reporting team or news outlet owns it and connects it to better public-interest reporting, a credible pilot/publication path, an expert/security review, and a tight timeline/budget. “Open-source” alone is not a stated criterion; do not present it as one.

## Investigative Journalism for Europe (IJ4EU)

**Documented program/selection evidence.** IJ4EU’s Investigation Support Scheme is for teams based in at least two eligible European countries, proposing cross-border public-interest investigations. Grants range €5,000–€50,000, with publication in respected news platforms in at least two eligible countries. Its criteria include cross-border relevance, European/local public relevance, newsworthiness and added value, research/publication plan, reach, feasibility/budget, credentials, risk assessment and urgency of need; the jury also seeks geographic/thematic/team balance. IJ4EU provides legal/editing support and a secure workspace based on hosted open-source tools after a digital-security assessment. [Scheme details](https://investigativejournalismforeu.net/grants/investigation-support-scheme/) [FAQ](https://investigativejournalismforeu.net/faq/)

**People.** The April 2024 announcement names five-person jury chair Carlos Dada for that round; it identifies IPI as scheme manager and consortium roles for EJC, ECPMF, and Arena. It does not identify selection responsibility per project. [2024 award round](https://investigativejournalismforeu.net/ij4eu-awards-e540000-to-17-cross-border-investigations/)

**Narrative implication.** This is the strongest journalism match for a security-sensitive collaboration tool, but the applicant still must be an eligible multi-country journalism team/news organization and foreground a cross-border investigation/publication plan. Describe the tool as time-bounded, risk-assessed reporting infrastructure; identify user research, digital-security review, publication partners, and how the team maintains editorial independence. These are criterion-aligned recommendations, not proof that any technical feature causes selection.

## Pulitzer Center

**Documented program/selection evidence.** The Machine Learning Reporting Grant welcomes NLP used to solve a data/reporting problem and asks for a concrete project, transparent/verified methodology, publication plan, code/data-sharing plan, budget, work samples/references and CV. It describes typical historic awards of $10,000–$25,000. General reporting grants fund hard costs of a specific story/series with outlet commitment, rather than broad concepts. The Data Journalism Grant similarly supports innovative data-driven journalism. The AI Accountability Fellowship page was closed for 2026–27 when checked; it was a cohort programme, not an open route. [ML grant](https://pulitzercenter.org/grants-fellowships/opportunities-journalists/machine-learning-reporting-grants) [general FAQ](https://pulitzercenter.org/applying-reporting-grant-frequently-asked-questions) [Data Journalism](https://pulitzercenter.org/grants-fellowships/opportunities-journalists/data-journalism-grants) [AI Accountability](https://pulitzercenter.org/grants-fellowships/opportunities-journalists/ai-accountability-fellowships)

**People.** The current public directory identifies Lisa Gibbs (CEO), Marina Walker Guevara (Executive Editor), Flora Pereira (Chief Impact Officer), Karol Ilagan and Joanna S. Kao (Information & AI editors), Jelter Meers (Data & Research lead), Ash Guevara (IT & Security Manager), Hannah Berk (Impact special initiatives), and Maria Karienova (AI Engagement program manager). Current roles do not show individual selection responsibility at a past award; no such claim is made. [Staff directory](https://pulitzercenter.org/about/staff)

**Narrative implication.** The credible path is a journalism-led, outlet-committed investigation where local pseudonymization enables a specific data/reporting method. Name the public-interest question, data/provenance and verification method, source-protection approach, editor/outlet, public engagement, feasible hard-cost budget, and a safe reproducibility plan. Do not pitch general software development, a startup, or basic equipment. This is a criterion-aligned inference, not evidence of selection causality.

## Limits that matter for scoring

1. Award listings are not proposal texts. They cannot reveal rejected alternatives, reviewer deliberation, hidden risk assessments, or causation.
2. Public pages often omit awards’ exact project scope, individual leadership and contemporaneous selection staff to protect reporting. These unknowns must remain null, not zero.
3. FIJ has direct U.S.-investigative-reporting and publication-commitment gates; JFE and IJ4EU have European/cross-border journalism eligibility gates. A standalone software developer is not presumptively eligible.
4. A similarity score ranks documented topical/workflow overlap, not funding likelihood. Eligibility and stated criteria must be gated separately.
