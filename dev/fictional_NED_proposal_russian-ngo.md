## 1. Organization and legal status

RFA is a nonpartisan civic association founded in 2022 by Russian journalists, lawyers and data analysts who relocated after independent outlets were blocked. It is registered with the Lithuanian Register of Legal Entities; the certificate of registration is attached.

RFA has 11 staff in Vilnius, Tbilisi and Yerevan and a vetted network of 46 contributors inside Russia. Since 2023 it has published 132 investigations on regional procurement and land deals, built a searchable database of 9,800 municipal contracts, and trained 210 citizen monitors in digital safety. RFA takes no funding from any government body, party or candidate.

## 2. Problem statement

Russian citizens have almost no safe way to learn how public money is spent where they live. Laws on "foreign agents" and "undesirable organizations" have shut most independent regional media. Officials' asset declarations were withdrawn from public view in late 2022, and much procurement and court data is now restricted or removed.

Corruption stories that do surface are often unverified, so they are easy to dismiss. People who witness abuses rarely know how to record evidence without exposing themselves. Open Ledger closes three gaps: access to verified local data, safe channels to publish it, and a secure way to document rights violations.

## 3. Goal, objectives and activities

**Goal:** strengthen citizen oversight of public spending and protect the public record of rights abuses in 12 Russian regions.

1. **Objective 1 — Open accountability data.** Expand the municipal contracts database to 12 regions and add archived asset and court records.
   - Scrape, archive and verify procurement records before they are removed (months 1–12).
   - Release quarterly "Regional Spending Briefs" that compare contract prices with market benchmarks.
2. **Objective 2 — Verified independent reporting.** Publish at least 48 fact-checked local investigations and explainers.
   - Commission work from 24 inside contributors, edited and legally reviewed in Tbilisi.
   - Distribute through a mirrored website, a Telegram channel, a YouTube channel and an email newsletter that work around blocking.
3. **Objective 3 — Safe rights documentation.** Train 120 citizen monitors to record and submit evidence of abuses securely.
   - Run 8 online courses and 2 in-person workshops in Yerevan on evidence standards and digital safety.
   - Maintain an encrypted intake channel; share anonymized case files with UN special procedures and established human rights archives.

## 5. Risks, security and nonpartisanship

The main risk is prosecution of contributors inside Russia, so RFA never publishes their names or locations. Contributors use pseudonyms, hardened devices and compartmentalized communication. An outside firm audits RFA's security twice a year, and an emergency fund covers legal aid and relocation.

If platforms are blocked, mirrored sites and alternative channels keep content available. Every investigation passes two-source verification and legal review before release.

Open Ledger is strictly nonpartisan and consistent with NED's Statement of Principles and Objectives. RFA does not endorse candidates or parties, and it reports on officials of every affiliation by the same standard.
