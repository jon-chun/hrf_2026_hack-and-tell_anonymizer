PORT MARISOL -- Eleven people were taken to hospital on Thursday after a minibus left the coast road near Hollin Creek and came to rest against a harbour wall, police said.

The vehicle, operated by Tidewater Shuttle Services, was carrying commuters to the ferry terminal when the accident happened at about 7.40 a.m. on 29 October 2026, according to a statement from Inspector Ingrid Haugen of the Marisol Regional Police.

"We are treating this as an accident for now," Haugen told reporters outside the station at 22 Quayside Road. "No one has died, and we are grateful to the bystanders."

The driver, a 54-year-old widow who runs the only pharmacy on Kestrel Street, was treated for shoulder injuries and released. Police gave her passport number as P8830421X when they opened the file on 29/10/2026, though she is not under suspicion.

Among the passengers was Mohammed al-Rashidi, 31, a ferry deckhand. Reuters earlier spelled his name Muhammad Al Rashid; his family says the first version is correct. "I heard a bang and then the glass went," he said by phone from St. Brendan's Hospital, which is run by the regional health authority. His cousin, Zhang Wei (listed on some rosters as Wei Zhang), said the bus had been late three days running.

Councillor Bartholomew "Bart" O'Rourke called for an inquiry. "This road has been a worry since the council's 2024 safety review," he said, adding that the European Union had earmarked funds for resurfacing which were never spent.

Tidewater's managing director, Fatima Zahra Benali, said the firm would cooperate fully. "Our thoughts are with everybody involved," she said in a message posted to the company's account, @TidewaterShuttle, on Friday morning.

Witnesses and anyone with dashcam footage are asked to call the police incident line on +61 7 5550 0188 or write to incidents@marisolpolice.example.gov.au. The United Nations road safety office in Geneva has been sent a copy of the preliminary report, a spokesperson said.

The road reopened on Saturday. A reporter, 4 November 2026.
