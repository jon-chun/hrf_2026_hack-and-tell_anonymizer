# Technology-funder research packet

Accessed 2026-11-04. The accompanying `technology.json` contains 57 structured, deduplicated award records: OTF 13, HRF BDF 16, OpenSats 8, Mozilla MTF 20. This is a documented sample, at or below the requested ceiling of 20 per funder, rather than an asserted complete historical inventory. It favors sources with a named project, purpose, and usable direct link.

## Evidence handling

An award announcement or project profile shows that support was public, and may describe funded work. It does **not** recover the application, identify a selection rationale, or establish a causal link between a feature and award. “0” feature values mean the sampled official source did not describe that feature; they are not proof it was absent. The six vectors are descriptive similarity signals only. They must not be combined with eligibility, budget, team capacity, or selection-process claims.

The individual named after each HRF award is a public project founder/developer/director where the official page supplies that role. OTF pages commonly identify an organization only. OpenSats and Mozilla entries often identify awardees but not a project lead. No award record attributes a decision to a named funder employee unless an official page explicitly says so. Mehan Jayasuriya is quoted as Mozilla Senior Program Officer in the 2024 announcement; that announcement does not establish that he selected any particular award. The current OpenSats application page says its board vets applications, but this does not establish membership or reviewers for 2025 awards.

## Open Technology Fund

OTF is the strongest subject-matter fit in this group for the technical concept described in the project workplan: its official reports define the Internet Freedom Fund as supporting technology development, implementation, applied research and digital security to counter censorship and repression. Its 2022 report also expressly says the Secure Usability and Accessibility Lab works on open-source circumvention/digital-security products for journalists and human-rights defenders. The documented portfolio includes OONI anonymous credentials, qaul, RCBox, dComms, Mapeo, Mailvelope, Awala and NetworkGenie.

The closest records are OONI (privacy-preserving contributor verification), Mapeo (local-first/offline human-rights documentation), dComms (self-hosted resilient communications for media/civil society), and RCBox (curated offline resources). A proposal should therefore name a digital-authoritarian or surveillance threat and an at-risk user group, explain local data handling and optional cloud boundaries precisely, and define tests for utility, usability, privacy exposure and deployment. That is an evidence-based fit hypothesis; it is not a documented scoring rule. OTF sources do not identify individual proposal reviewers or project leads for most of these records.

Primary sources: [program report](https://www.opentech.fund/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/OTF-Annual-Report-FY-2022.pdf), [OONI](https://www.opentech.fund/projects-we-support/supported-projects/ooni-anonymous-credentials/), [qaul](https://www.opentech.fund/projects-we-support/supported-projects/qual-net/), [RCBox](https://www.opentech.fund/projects-we-support/supported-projects/building-resilient-community-and-communications-fiesta-mantequilla/), [dComms](https://www.opentech.fund/projects-we-support/supported-projects/dcomms/), [NetworkGenie](https://www.opentech.fund/projects-we-support/supported-projects/networkgenie-root-level-packet-manipulation-on-non-rooted-mobile-devices/).

## HRF Bitcoin Development Fund

HRF BDF publicly frames its grants around Bitcoin and related freedom technology for human-rights defenders in politically and financially challenging environments. The sampled May 2025 award announcement states support for open-source development, education, mining decentralization and privacy tools. The closest privacy/civic matches in that announcement are Stringer News (a Bitcoin/Nostr media platform, founded by Anjan Sundaram), Payjoin work by developer spacebear, SeedSigner work led by Keith Mukai, the FROST work at Blockchain Commons, and ecash UX work led by Erik Cativo. The announcement also names Bryan Bishop, thunderbiscuit, Mike Schmidt, Peter Van Valkenburgh and Bitcoin Design Foundation’s three founding designers in other awards.

It supports a narrowly scoped positioning only where the proposal genuinely advances Bitcoin, Nostr, ecash, financial privacy, or a closely connected freedom technology. A generic PII-anonymization product would not gain fit merely by using “privacy” language. If relevant, describe a concrete monetary-surveillance or censorship threat, how the work improves user safety or access under repression, its open-source deliverable, and adoption/education path. Individual grant amounts, application text, and HRF decision staff are not disclosed in the sampled announcement.

Primary sources: [BDF grantee program page](https://hrf.org/program/financial-freedom/bitcoin-development-fund/grantees/), [May 2025 award round](https://hrf.org/latest/hrf-grants-800-million-satoshis-to-22-freedom-tech-projects-worldwide/), [five-year report](https://hrf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025_HRF_Financial-Freedom_BDF-5-Year-Report.pdf).

## OpenSats

OpenSats offers the most explicit public application rules in this group: direct Bitcoin impact, free/open-source work with an appropriate license, and transparency/education. It asks for concise, informative applications, says a strong one fits on one page, accepts applicants worldwide and nym-friendly, and requires public access/edit/redistribution of funded code and documents. Its general grant is for high-impact Bitcoin work; its LTS awards target critical Bitcoin maintainers, and its RED reimbursement supports security research.

The sampled 2025 fifteenth wave includes privacy-preserving or self-hostable work (BlindBit Suite), grassroots Lightning infrastructure (LaWallet), Bitcoin security research/vault work (LNHANCE), and decentralized mining/accounting work (Braidpool, developer Mohd Zaid). Those are direct Bitcoin projects with identifiable public artifacts. OpenSats is an implausible target for a proposal whose connection to Bitcoin and public open-source distribution is incidental. If it is in scope, the proposal should meet the three stated criteria with visible repositories, license, clear milestones, and a public education/knowledge-sharing commitment; do not imply OpenSats funds private or closed-source tooling.

Primary sources: [application criteria](https://opensats.org/apply), [transparency page](https://opensats.org/transparency), [fifteenth wave](https://opensats.org/blog/fifteenth-wave-of-bitcoin-grants).

## Mozilla Technology Fund

Mozilla’s MTF is a themed open-source grant program, rather than a persistent open fund for any privacy tool. Its public 2022 cohort concerned AI bias/transparency, 2023 AI audit tools, and 2024 open-source AI/environmental justice. The 2024 announcement says awards were up to $50,000 with one year of mentorship/support. Its featured projects include open data/tooling and public-interest outcomes: Amazon Mining Watch exposes illegal mining via public web/GitHub outputs; PUDL makes reproducible public energy data; AirQo deploys air-quality information to community-facing platforms; and the 2022–23 list includes TikTok Observatory, AI Forensics, Crossover, Gigbox and Zeno.

For future Mozilla calls, do not recycle the 2024 environmental narrative unless it is the call’s theme. A plausible narrative must lead with the published theme, a genuine open-source artifact, a public-interest community outcome, and transparent evaluation. Current application requirements and people must be checked when a live call is selected. The record does not identify individual selection committee members; Mozilla’s MTF GitHub says it keeps project and committee information, but the archive should be read contemporaneously before asserting a committee role.

Primary sources: [MTF archive](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/sw/what-we-fund/programs/mozilla-technology-fund-mtf/previous-awards/), [2024 cohort announcement](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/blog/open-source-AI-for-environmental-justice/), [MTF directory](https://github.com/MozillaFoundation/mozilla-technology-fund), [Fellowship page](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/what-we-do/grantmaking/fellowship/).

## Integration notes

* Apply no “success factor” weight from this packet alone. It contains positive cases and no declined, applicant-pool, or reviewed-proposal data. Weight documented **program requirements** separately from descriptive project similarity.
* The supplied technical-spec baseline is a stronger input than the provisional background concept: local detection, reversible keyed surrogates, and optional cloud QA on pseudonymized text. This makes OTF’s fit more direct. It gives a partial—not full—offline fit because a cloud step remains optional.
* Score record similarity only where a project page describes a relevant feature; publish evidence coverage beside each score. Use distinct, live program rules when producing target-specific revision advice.
