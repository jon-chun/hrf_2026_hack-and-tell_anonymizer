# Democracy and rights funders — primary-source research

Research date/access date: 2026-11-04. This stream covers Civil Rights Defenders (CRD), Freedom House, European Endowment for Democracy (EED), and National Endowment for Democracy (NED). It is a bounded public-evidence sample, not a reconstruction of private applications, committee deliberations, or confidential grantee identities. Search strategy: official funder domains only; first application/programme criteria, then annual reports, public grant registers, project/partner stories and current leadership pages; duplicates excluded by project title; maximum 20 funded-project records per funder. The structured companion is `democracy.json`.

## Civil Rights Defenders

CRD’s Emergency Fund is a documented high-fit channel for an at-risk defender’s immediate security need, though it is not publicly documented as an open technology-grant competition. It provides rapid targeted assistance including legal aid, temporary relocation, security training and digital protection. CRD reports support to 2,302 defenders and family members in 29 countries during 2025; its Emergency Fund page reports 1,459 defenders in 34 countries during 2024. The 2025 report identifies Ana Maria Mendoza as Emergency Fund Programme Lead, but does not establish her role in individual award selection.

CRD’s broader programmes include the Natalia Project security alarm and the Defenders’ Database, which helps defenders document, analyse and visualize rights violations. The technical concept is relevant where it can be deployed for a named partner’s urgent digital-security threat or safely handling testimonies for accountability work. Make the threat, beneficiary, rapid deployment path and continued-work outcome specific. General product R&D is not shown to fit Emergency Fund support. CRD publishes aggregate activity and selected support stories but no project-level grant register or open selection rubric; no supposed grant recipients have been inferred from protected cases.

Sources: <https://crd.org/emergency-fund/>, <https://crd.org/global-programs/>, <https://crd.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2025-Verksamhetsberattelse-och-arsredovisning-pa-engelska.pdf>.

## Freedom House

Freedom House’s Emergency Assistance Program is fundamentally a threat-response mechanism. Its published eligible categories include security, relocation, medical and legal support, humanitarian assistance, and replacement of confiscated or damaged equipment. It also describes short rapid grants for advocacy/opportunity and resilience/security support. The Dignity programme says it independently verifies requests and applies eligibility/justification review.

The public record reports high aggregate volume but protects case identities: this is ethically material evidence, not a reason to infer recipients. For a local pseudonymization tool, fit only exists if a named at-risk HRD/CSO has an immediate, substantiated security need and a short deliverable. General OSS development, research, or a long product roadmap should not be presented as emergency relief.

Relevant named staff evidence is descriptive only: Jerusha Burnham is identified as former Emergency Assistance Programs director in a 2025 perspective, and Andrea Martalo as an EAP program officer in a 2024 article. Neither item documents their role in individual selections.

Sources: <https://freedomhouse.org/pl/node/464>, <https://freedomhouse.org/fa/node/483>, <https://freedomhouse.org/ko/node/7585>, <https://freedomhouse.org/article/guatemalans-have-spoken-they-choose-democracy>.

## European Endowment for Democracy

EED is the strongest programmatic match among this stream only if the applicant and beneficiaries are rooted in its eligible neighbourhood geography. It accepts requests continuously, may support individuals, informal groups and operational costs, and positions itself as a gap-filler for actors who cannot use normal EU instruments. Its Executive Committee makes final decisions after Secretariat assessment; emergency treatment requires a genuine unforeseen crisis and consequences of delay. It requires a budget template and asks for three references.

The 20-record sample favors locally embedded independent media, accountability reporting, civic participation, survival and adaptation under repression or funding shocks. Named records include Citizens.al, Shteg, NYJE, Amfora, Gjurmon, Syrians for Truth and Justice, Project 64, RTV Slon, TV8, De Facto/Trigger, MOST, Campji, Al-Jumhuriya and the February Network. Six Emergency Mechanism records retain EED’s anonymized project titles to avoid attempting to identify the protected partner. Only two public leadership names were available in the sample: Lorin Kadiu (Citizens.al executive director) and Mariana Rață (TV8 co-founder); these are project-side roles, not evidence of selection relationships.

Proposal implications: start with a geographically eligible local partner and the partner’s stated threat/problem; establish why conventional support cannot fund it; ask for a proportionate core/implementation budget; make privacy and preservation of independent reporting a route to accountable civic participation. Avoid an unsupported global-technology framing. The technical feature profile is closest to documentation, investigative media, and communities exposed to repression; the public sample has no evidence for reversible pseudonymization as a recurring award feature.

Sources: <https://www.democracyendowment.eu/support>, <https://democracyendowment.eu/annualreport2025/i-albania-supporting-democratic-practices>, <https://www.democracyendowment.eu/annualreport2024/special3.html>, <https://www.democracyendowment.eu/annualreport2024/special5.html>, <https://democracyendowment.eu/archive/albina-vickovic-and-rtv-slon>, <https://democracyendowment.eu/annualreport2025/iii-moldova-democracy-strikes-back>, <https://democracyendowment.eu/country-updates/moldova-a-shifting-landscape-in-pre-election-times-and-nbsp>, <https://democracyendowment.eu/stories/community-media>.

## National Endowment for Democracy

NED exposes the clearest direct pathway in this group: its application page requires an organizational profile, project form, narrative, corresponding budget and registration record or explanation; it accepts local-language narratives through its regional portal. Its 2025 public materials emphasize local democratic agency, independent media, open information, secure communication, digital resilience and civic technology.

The 20-record official sample’s closest technical comparators are **Protecting Civil Society Access to a Free Internet and Digital Rights** ($550,000), **Promoting Anticorruption Mechanisms** ($80,000), and **Strengthening the Digital Security and Resilience of Civil Society** ($150,000). They respectively cover open-source privacy/circumvention resources and training, a secure open-source whistleblower system, and security assessments/training. Other entered projects connect civic documentation, secure independent media, information access, data journalism, public-spending transparency and anti-authoritarian technology. Their recipient identities and project leaders are intentionally absent from the public listing.

Proposal implications: lead with a particular country/region and locally accountable civil-society or independent-media users; identify a threat model; show how privacy-preserving handling enables verifiable civic information or investigative work; specify adoption, training, maintenance and measurable use; and make every cost trace to an activity. Do not claim the publicly visible awards prove causation or that NED has funded reversible pseudonymization. They establish a documented adjacency to secure civic information workflows and open-source digital rights resources.

Sources: <https://www.ned.org/apply-for-grant/en/>, <https://www.ned.org/national-endowment-for-democracy-2025-annual-report/>, <https://www.ned.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FINAL-Active-Grant-Listing-7-15-For-Listing-1.pdf>, <https://www.ned.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2025-Annual-Report.pdf>, <https://www.ned.org/active-grant-listing/>.

## Similarity coding notes

The six columns in `democracy.json` use the baseline defined in `WORKPLAN.md`: privacy/security tooling; local/offline operation; reversible/consistent pseudonymization; long-form/journalistic/civic text workflow; vulnerable civic users; OSS/public-good distribution. Scores are textual feature evidence (0 no feature described, 1 adjacent, 2 explicit; `null` insufficient evidence), not quality scores and not grant-selection probabilities. NED’s secure-submission and digital-rights awards are the closest observed records in this stream. EED’s named media records are close on civic narrative and vulnerable audiences, but do not document pseudonymization or offline operation.
